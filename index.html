What this estimate tells you, and what it doesn't

The calculator follows each missed call down a simple funnel: missed, not recovered, a real lead, a job you would have closed, and the revenue that job would have brought in. It shows you the size of the leak using your own numbers. It can't tell you how many of those calls you would actually win back with any particular fix. The estimate is only as accurate as the percentages you type in, so it's worth measuring them (see below) instead of guessing.

How the math works

lost revenue per month = calls × missed% × no-callback% × lead% × close% × average job value

Annual lost revenue is the monthly figure × 12. Break-even jobs per month = your monthly cost ÷ average job value (only if you enter one).

How to measure your own missed-call rate

  1. Pull your call logs. Most phone carriers and VoIP systems let you export call history showing answered, missed, and voicemail calls. Export at least two full weeks.
  2. Count answered vs. unanswered. Missed% = unanswered inbound calls ÷ all inbound calls. Count calls that rang out and calls sent to voicemail as missed.
  3. Check voicemail volume. Compare the number of voicemails left with the number of missed calls. The gap is roughly the callers who hung up without leaving a message.
  4. Track callbacks. For the same two weeks, note which missed numbers called back or that you reached later. That gives you your no-callback%.
  5. Tag lead calls. Mark which calls were new-job inquiries, as opposed to vendors, spam, or existing customers, to estimate lead%.
  6. Use your own close rate. Your CRM, job board, or even a spreadsheet of estimates sent vs. jobs won works.

Measure during both a busy stretch and a slow one. Missed calls tend to bunch up when crews are on jobsites, after hours, and during storm seasons.

FAQ

Is this accurate?

The math is simple multiplication, so it's exact for the numbers you enter. How close the result is to reality depends on your inputs. Use numbers from your call logs and job records wherever you can, and treat the output as an estimate, not a forecast.

What's a normal missed-call rate for contractors?

We deliberately don't quote an "average" here. We couldn't find a primary-source figure we'd be comfortable citing, and rates vary a lot by trade, crew size, and season. Your own call log over two weeks will tell you far more than an industry number would.

Does text-back work?

It can help reach callers who hang up without leaving a voicemail, because it opens a conversation they can answer when they're free. How well it works for you depends on how quickly someone follows up. The best way to find out is to measure it: compare your no-callback% before and after you turn it on.

Is my data stored or sent anywhere?

No. Everything is calculated in your browser with plain JavaScript. The page has no analytics, no cookies, no tracking scripts, and no form submission to a server. You can read the full source code in the repository.
